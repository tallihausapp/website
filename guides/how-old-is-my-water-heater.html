How old is my water heater?

The age is almost always on the label on the side of the tank. Sometimes it's printed as a date. More often it's hidden in the serial number, and every brand hides it differently.

Find the label

Look on the side of the tank for a sticker with the model and serial number. On gas heaters it's usually near the bottom, above the burner cover. Use a flashlight and your phone's camera if it's tight against a wall.

If you see MFG Date or Date of Manufacture, you're done. If not, find the serial number and use the table below.

Where the date is, by brand

BrandHow the date is writtenExample
Rheem, Ruud, RichmondFirst 4 digits: month, then year0619… = June 2019
A.O. Smith, State (2008 on)First 4 digits: year, then week1912… = March 2019
American, U.S. Craftmaster, WhirlpoolFirst 4 digits: year, then week1745… = November 2017
Bradford WhiteFirst letter = year, second letter = monthXC… = March 2021

Weeks to months: weeks 1–4 are January, 5–8 February, 9–13 March, 14–17 April, 18–22 May, 23–26 June, 27–30 July, 31–35 August, 36–39 September, 40–44 October, 45–48 November, 49–52 December.

How long do they last?

A regular tank water heater usually lasts about 8 to 12 years. Hard water, a lot of hot water use and never flushing the tank shorten that. Tankless heaters often go 20 years.

Past 10 years, start watching for rusty hot water, rumbling or popping when it heats, and any water around the base. A leak at the bottom of the tank can't be fixed, so it's worth replacing on your schedule rather than after it floods the basement.
