How old is my furnace or air conditioner?

Check the metal rating plate first. Many furnaces and AC units print the date right on it. If not, the serial number has it, coded a little differently by each brand.

Where the label is

  • Furnace: take off the front panel (it usually lifts off, no tools). The plate is on the inside wall of the cabinet. Turn the furnace off at the switch first.
  • Outdoor AC or heat pump: on the side of the unit, often near the electrical box where the lines go in.

Look for MFG Date or Manufacture Date. If it's there, that's your answer.

Where the date is, by brand

BrandHow the date is writtenExample
Carrier, Bryant, PayneFirst 4 digits: week, then year3216… = August 2016
Trane, American Standard (2010 on)First 4 digits: year, then week1924… = June 2019
LennoxDigits 3 and 4 = year, then a letter for the month5819K… = October 2019
Goodman, Amana, JanitrolFirst 4 digits: year, then month1905… = May 2019
Rheem, RuudFour digits a few characters in: week, then year…4418… = November 2018

Weeks to months: weeks 1–4 are January, 5–8 February, 9–13 March, 14–17 April, 18–22 May, 23–26 June, 27–30 July, 31–35 August, 36–39 September, 40–44 October, 45–48 November, 49–52 December.

How long do they last?

SystemTypical life
Gas furnace15–25 years
Central air conditioner7–15 years
Heat pump10–15 years

Ranges from InterNACHI's life expectancy chart. Yearly service and regular filter changes push a system toward the long end.
