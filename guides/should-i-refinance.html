Should I refinance my mortgage?

Rate rules of thumb are a starting point. The real question is how long it takes to earn back what the refinance costs, and whether you'll still be in the house by then.

The break-even math

closing costs ÷ monthly savings = months to break even

Say refinancing costs $4,000 and lowers your payment by $200 a month. It takes 20 months to earn the $4,000 back. Every month after that is real savings. If you might move in a year, it's not worth it. If you plan to stay ten years, it probably is.

Closing costs often run 2% to 5% of the loan amount, so get a Loan Estimate from a lender to see the real number.

Watch the loan term

If you're 7 years into a 30-year loan and refinance into a new 30-year loan, you lower the payment partly by stretching it back out to 30 years. You can end up paying more interest overall even at a lower rate. A 20- or 15-year loan sometimes keeps the payment close to what you pay now and saves a lot more.

Other things to check

  • Whether PMI would come off (or come back) with the new loan
  • "No-closing-cost" offers, which usually mean a slightly higher rate instead
  • Your credit score, since the best advertised rates go to the highest scores
