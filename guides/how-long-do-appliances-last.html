How long do appliances last?

Most of the big things in a house wear out on a fairly predictable schedule. If you know how old yours are, you can see the next big expense coming.

Appliance or systemTypical life
Tank water heater8–12 years
Tankless water heaterabout 20 years
Gas furnace15–25 years
Central air conditioner7–15 years
Heat pump10–15 years
Refrigeratorabout 13 years
Dishwasherabout 9 years
Washing machineabout 10 years
Dryerabout 13 years
Range or oven13–15 years
Microwaveabout 9 years
Garbage disposalabout 12 years

HVAC ranges from InterNACHI's life expectancy chart; appliance figures from the National Association of Home Builders' study of home component life. Use matters: a big family's washer won't last as long as a single person's.

Don't know how old yours are?

The age is on the label, sometimes as a printed date and sometimes hidden in the serial number:

  • Water heaters
  • Furnaces and air conditioners
  • GE appliances

Getting near the end?

An appliance past its typical life isn't broken, but it's worth a little planning: set some money aside, write down the model number so you can compare replacements, and don't sink a big repair bill into something that's already old. A common rule of thumb is to replace rather than repair when the repair costs more than half the price of a new one.
